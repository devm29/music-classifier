How it works

The pipeline

An upload is decoded (ffmpeg via pydub for anything that is not a WAV), averaged to mono, truncated to the first {{ model.clip_seconds|default:30|floatformat:0 }} seconds and resampled to {{ model.sample_rate|default:22050 }} Hz. Thirteen MFCCs are computed over that clip and summarised as 13 means plus the 13 upper diagonals of their 13×13 covariance — 104 numbers. A standardised, probability-calibrated RBF support-vector machine scores them.

Why the resampling step matters

MFCCs are a function of the sample rate: the mel filterbank runs to rate / 2 and frames are winlen × rate samples long. The training features were extracted from GTZAN at 22,050 Hz. Handed the same waveform at 44,100 Hz — which is what essentially every MP3 is — the feature vector lands about 0.47 training standard deviations from its own 22,050 Hz version. Resampling first brings that to 0.07. Without it, every real-world upload was scored off-distribution.

What the confidence band means

The served model is roughly 71% accurate out of sample, so the top guess is wrong about one time in four. A result is called confident only when the top probability clears 40% and beats the runner-up by 10 points; anything else is reported as uncertain, with the reason. A narrow two-way split is a tie, not an answer.

…and what off-distribution means

A support-vector machine has no idea what it has not seen. Handed audio unlike anything in GTZAN — a synthesised tone, a podcast, near-silence — it does not abstain: it returns its off-distribution default, which for this model is jazz at about 54%, blues second, for essentially any such input. Rendered as “confident: jazz”, that would be the most misleading thing this page could say.

So the nearest-neighbour lookup gates the verdict. A real GTZAN track’s closest neighbour sits at a standardised distance of about 6.9 — 95% are under 12.8 — and the widest 5% of pairs of real tracks are about 21 apart. When fewer than 5% of pairs are further apart than a clip’s closest training track, the verdict reads off-distribution and says the model is extrapolating.

It discriminates rather than just flagging anything synthetic. Of the three seeded demo clips, the distorted riff lands 6.6 from metal.00004 and is scored normally; the arpeggio lands 24.2 and the drum loop 65.1, and both are flagged. A sustained sine tone, for what it is worth, lands about 9.9 from a quiet classical track and is not flagged.

What it cannot do

  • It knows ten genres and nothing else. Handed a podcast, it will confidently name one of them.
  • GTZAN has documented duplicates, mislabelled tracks and artist bleed. Accuracy on it is not accuracy on real music.
  • The per-window timeline uses 10-second windows against a model fitted on 30-second summaries, so its covariance estimates are noisier. Read it as a texture-change indicator, not a second opinion.
  • Nearest neighbours are named by GTZAN's filename convention recovered from the row index, not from stored titles.
